# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Peers, co-investors, collaborators, fellow founders, and the merely curious — people who land on lsd.capital to work out who LSD Capital actually is. They are not filling in a form or completing a task; they're forming an impression. Their job to be done is to quickly grasp the ethos (heart-led, hands-on), see tangible proof that it's real (products shipped, founders backed, a community shown up for), and feel invited to reach out directly rather than through a gate.

The site is read most often by someone deciding whether LSD is the kind of partner, backer, or peer worth an email — so the design's job is to make "these are our people" land in the first screen.

## Product Purpose

LSD Capital is a tiny product studio that also invests, based in Cape Town and Seychelles. The site exists to tell one unified story: they build the things they wish existed (LinkiDink, itwyit, ficta) **and** back the people building the rest (18 angel/seed founders, Africa & beyond), plus show up in community (the NDL theme camp at Afrikaburn). "Some weeks we ship. Some weeks we invest. It's all the same instinct."

Success is a visitor who understands the dual build-and-invest identity as one instinct rather than two businesses, trusts the low-ego and human approach, and emails `hello@lsd.capital` — "We read everything ourselves. No forms, no associates, no black hole."

## Brand Personality

Heart-led, hands-on, quiet. The voice is humble, personal, and first-person plural — understated to the point of deliberately underselling ("shipped quietly, no fanfare"; "in for the long middle"). It should feel like a person, not an institution: warm, sincere, and confident without volume. Emotional goals are warmth, trust, and a sense of genuine craft — the opposite of transactional finance.

## Anti-references

- **Slick corporate VC.** Navy-and-gold palettes, glossy fund sites, hero-metric templates ("$50M AUM", "40+ portfolio companies" as the headline), stock photography of handshakes and skylines, and contact flows gated behind associates or forms. The whole institutional-finance costume.
- **The old cool-paper newspaper/editorial fund look.** The previous site's Fraunces serif, drop-caps, `rule-draw` masthead animation, and "Vol. 01 / MMXXVI · § Section" motifs were deliberately dropped in the warm redesign. Don't reintroduce them.
- **Generic startup / SaaS slop.** Gradient-heavy heroes, identical icon-heading-text card grids repeated down the page, and tiny tracked-uppercase eyebrows above every section.

## Design Principles

- **Show, don't tell.** Prove the ethos with shipped products, named founders, and a real camp in the desert — never with adjectives about how caring or hands-on the team is.
- **Build and back are one instinct.** The page must hold both halves of the identity without ever reading like two separate companies bolted together.
- **Human over institutional.** First-person voice, a direct `mailto:` instead of a contact form, no gatekeeping. Every interaction should feel like reaching a person.
- **Quiet confidence.** Restraint and craft signal seriousness here; there is no metrics theatre and no shouting. The design earns trust by being calm and precise.
- **Warmth lives in palette, type, and copy — not decoration.** The brand's warmth is carried by the sand background, the rounded warm type, and the sincere voice, so ornament stays minimal.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**. Body text holds ≥4.5:1 contrast against its background; large/display text ≥3:1. Every animation ships a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative. The site is fully keyboard-navigable with visible focus states, and all logos and meaningful images carry alt text. Because the brand green (`#1f9442`) is the primary accent, it must never be the sole carrier of meaning — pair it with text, shape, or position so color-blind and low-vision users lose nothing.
